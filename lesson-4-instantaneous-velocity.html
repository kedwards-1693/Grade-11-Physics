Instantaneous velocity: how fast, right now

Average velocity over a stretch versus velocity at a single moment, and how to find it from a curve with a tangent line. About 60 minutes, plus a car-ride activity.

  • The SPH3U Formula Sheet from Google Classroom

1The speedometer and the radar gun

A car trip of 120 km takes 2 hours. Your average speed was 60 km/h. But if you glanced at the speedometer at one moment, it might have read 100 km/h. At a red light it read 0. Neither is “wrong.” They answer different questions.

A police radar gun does not measure your average speed over the trip. It measures your speed at the instant the beam hits you. That is the idea of instantaneous velocity: how fast, and which way, right now.

2Average versus instantaneous

In plain words

Average velocity is the slope of the straight line joining two points on a position-time graph. It covers a stretch of time.

Instantaneous velocity is the velocity at one moment. On a position-time graph it is the slope of the tangent line: a straight line that touches the curve at just that point and does not cut across it.

For a straight-line graph, the two are the same everywhere: a constant velocity is the same at every instant. For a curve, they differ. Try the slider. A player’s position is d = t². Shrink the time gap from 3.0 s and watch the average velocity settle towards the instantaneous velocity at 3.0 s.

Time intervalAverage velocity
3.0 s to 5.0 s8.0 m/s
3.0 s to 4.0 s7.0 m/s
3.0 s to 3.5 s6.5 m/s
3.0 s to 3.1 s6.1 m/s
at 3.0 s exactly6.0 m/s (the tangent’s slope)

As the gap shrinks, the line joining the two points swings round until it just touches the curve. That final line is the tangent.

A ball thrown straight up. A is on the way up, B is the top, C is on the way down.

Look at the ball’s curve. At A the tangent slopes steeply upward: the ball is moving up fast. At B, the top, the tangent is flat: the velocity is zero for an instant. At C the tangent slopes downward: the velocity is negative, because the ball is moving down.

Watch out

The ball’s height-time curve is not the path. The ball goes straight up and down. The curve shows how its height changes with time.

3Finding it: draw a tangent, then take its slope

  1. Find the point on the curve (the time you are asked about).
  2. Lay a clear ruler so that it touches the curve only at that point and balances there. The curve should be on one side just before the point and on the other side just after, with the ruler between.
  3. Draw the straight line.
  4. Pick two points on the tangent line, far apart, on grid crossings. They will not be on the curve.
  5. Slope = rise ÷ run = Δd ÷ Δt. That is the instantaneous velocity.

A tangent drawn at P (4 s). The two marked points on the red line are what you use for the slope.

Worked example 1: find the instantaneous velocity at 4.0 s from the tangent in the graph above.

Given: two points on the tangent line, (2.0 s, 0 m) and (6.0 s, 32 m). Find: velocity at 4.0 s.
Step 1. Rise. Δd = 32 − 0 = 32 m.
Step 2. Run. Δt = 6.0 − 2.0 = 4.0 s.
Step 3. Divide. v = 32 ÷ 4.0 = 8.0 m/s.
If you used the point P (4 s, 16 m) with the origin, you would get 16 ÷ 4 = 4.0 m/s. That is the average velocity over the first 4 s, not the instantaneous velocity at 4 s.
Step 4. Sense check. The curve is getting steeper, so the velocity at 4 s should be bigger than the average over 0 to 4 s (4.0 m/s). 8.0 is bigger. ✓
Watch out

Take the slope using two points on the tangent line, and not points on the curve. Points on the curve give you an average velocity over that stretch.

Worked example 2: a ball’s height-time curve has a flat tangent at its highest point. What is the instantaneous velocity there, and what does it mean?

Given: the tangent at the top is horizontal.
Step 1. Slope of a flat line. The rise is 0, so the slope is 0.
Step 2. Velocity. v = 0 m/s at that instant.
Step 3. Meaning. The ball stops rising for an instant before it starts falling.
Even though the velocity is zero at the top, the ball is still accelerating there. You will see that in Lesson 7 on free fall.

5In the real world, and hands-on

Soccer. A shot on goal might average 20 m/s over its flight, but at the instant it leaves the boot it is faster, because air slows it down. A shot-speed radar reads the instantaneous speed at one moment.

Speed cameras. Many measure two quick positions a very short time apart. The gap is so small that the average over it is basically the instantaneous speed. That is exactly the slider idea.

On a car ride (passenger only)
  1. At the start of a trip, note the odometer or trip distance and the clock time. Your partner can use a phone map for distance.
  2. During the ride, write down the speedometer reading at three different moments (for example at a stop, on a side road, on a faster road).
  3. At the end, find the trip distance and the trip time. Work out the average speed in km/h.
  4. Compare. Which of your three readings were instantaneous speeds? Did any match the average?
